You've seen 3D reconstructions. What happens when you change where you listen?

The recent Astra demos got us thinking about another part of realistic scenes: sound. We built AV3D-Harness to make audio-visual reconstruction an explicit, measurable loop.

Starting with an in-the-wild recording and a reconstructed scene, it:
• perceives the audible events;
• uses semantic descriptions to separate audio tracks;
• proposes possible source locations from visual assets;
• renders and compares acoustic hypotheses;
• rerenders the same moment along three new listening paths.

Watch with headphones—the ending lets you hear each route in turn.

The initial evaluator uses energy and left/right balance rather than an opaque learned score. Every candidate and parameter choice leaves a receipt. Fitted trajectories can also supply candidate training labels, with validation before any future training loop.

This is a research prototype: the retained speech layer still contains room background, and source identity is not independently verified. The code includes a reproducible runner, agent instructions, tool contracts, and the demo builder.

Built with Gemini, SAM Audio, Habitat and SoundSpaces. Video source: Nymeria-Plus.

If you're working on embodied or omnimodal systems, what would you add to the evaluator beyond energy and stereo balance?
